## Against Conventional Cuteness

There's a particular kind of plush that doesn't concern itself with being adorable. It tries to be _interesting_. Buggy eyes slightly too far apart. Proportions that suggest the designer was working from a description rather than a reference photo. An expression that says, "I have stories, and none of them are appropriate for children."

I like those.

This isn't a hobby I planned. It started with a fly in a German shop and spiraled from there. Six years later, my room looks like a support group for stuffed animals that didn't quite pass the focus testing phase. They're perfect.

### Bloody Mary: The One That Got Away (Twice)

![framed: The fly plushy Bloody Mary](images/optimized/blogs/2026_01_10_plushies/DSCF9999.webp)

I was eleven, wandering through Rothenburg ob der Tauber with my parents, when I spotted her in the window. A fly. Not a cartoon fly, not a cute fly. A fly that looked like it had spent too long at a bar. Bulging eyes. Scraggly fur. A vibe of chaotic energy.

She came from [Beasttown by Sigikid](https://sigikid.de/Beasts/), a brand where every plush has a name, a backstory, and a vaguely unsettling charm. I had to have her.

For a few months, she went everywhere with me. Trips, car rides, probably places a stuffed fly had no business being at. Then one day, she was gone. Left on my parents' car, driven away, never seen again. I was devastated in the way only an eleven-year-old who lost a beloved fly plush can be.

Months later, my mom's friend found her on eBay. Same fly. Same unhinged energy. While her eyes differ slightly, the reunion was emotional nevertheless.

### GuluGulu: The Hospital Unicorn

![framed: GuluGulu the unicorn with characteristic spiral eyes](images/optimized/blogs/2026_01_10_plushies/DSCF9779.webp)

Her original name was Fluffy. I renamed her GuluGulu because "Fluffy" felt too obvious for a unicorn with spiral eyes and the expression of someone who just discovered space travel.

GuluGulu is the Despicable Me unicorn, the one Agnes clutches and screams "IT'S SO FLUFFY I'M GONNA DIE" about. My then-girlfriend bought her at Universal Singapore with no intention of giving her away.

That changed when I got hospitalized for a collapsed lung.

There's something about lying in a hospital bed, tubes in places tubes shouldn't be, that makes you appreciate the small comforts. - And to my girlfriend, for understanding that sometimes a unicorn is non-negotiable

I got attached. She noticed. The loan became permanent.

Now GuluGulu wanders around my room, still looking mildly deranged, still reminding me that sometimes the best gifts are the ones people didn't plan to give.

### Taco Tuesday: The International Import

![framed: Taco Tuesday, the Beasttown dog with wild fur](images/optimized/blogs/2026_01_10_plushies/DSCF9996.webp)

After rediscovering Beasttown through the Bloody Mary replacement, I fell down a rabbit hole. Their website is dangerous. Every plush has a personality. Every one of them looks like it would have opinions about your life choices.

Taco Tuesday is a dog in the loosest sense of the word. He has the energy of a creature who chased too many cars and caught one. Wild black and white fur. Eyes that don't quite agree on what they're looking at. The kind of face that says, "I know where you hid the snacks, and I'm keeping that information for myself."

He shipped from Germany to Taiwan. International delivery for a plush that looks like it just survived a wind tunnel. Worth every penny, albeit, a lot of pennies.

### The Jellycat Trio: Organized Chaos

![framed: The Jellycat collection sitting in a row](images/optimized/blogs/2026_01_10_plushies/DSCF9998.webp)

Jellycat occupies a strange space in the plush world. They're a legitimate, well-respected brand. They make objectively cute things. And yet, some of their designs have... energy.

The trio on the couch represents the Jellycat aesthetic at its finest: roundish creatures with tiny limbs, bewildered expressions, and the posture of beings who aren't entirely sure how they ended up here. The green one looks perpetually confused. The blue one seems to be questioning reality. The yellow one, my favorite, radiates an utter acceptance of the absurd.

They sit together like a council of beings who've seen too much and decided to simply vibe about it.

### Why These?

Some may ask why I don't collect "normal" plushies. The kind with symmetrical features and predictable cuteness. The kind you'd find at any gift shop, indistinguishable from a thousand others.

Here's the thing: those plushies are fine. But they're designed to be liked. These weirdos are designed to be _noticed_. Each one has a story, a journey, a reason they ended up with me specifically.

Bloody Mary taught me that losing something doesn't mean losing it forever. GuluGulu reminds me that kindness often comes in unexpected forms. Taco Tuesday proves that international shipping costs are always justified when the alternative is not owning Taco Tuesday. The Jellycat trio just makes me smile every time I look at them, which is more than enough.

Maybe that's the real appeal of unconventional plushies. They don't fit a mold. They don't try to please everyone. They just exist, gloriously weird, waiting for someone to recognize that their particular flavor of chaos is exactly what was missing.
